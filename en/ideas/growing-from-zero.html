Growing from zero: what the first 1,000 customers teach you

How to build a fintech’s growth foundation across three countries, and why your first customers shape everything that follows.

When I joined Clara as the first Growth hire, there was no growth team. There was a product, a regional ambition, and a to-do list too big for one person. The job was to build the foundation: launch operations in Mexico, Brazil and Colombia, and do it at the same time across PR, communications, customer experience, customer success, marketing and sales.

That sounds like chaos, and partly it is. But it has an advantage you lose later: at the start the whole funnel lives in one head. You see how a press story moves a sales conversation, how a bad support experience kills a referral, how one well-served customer brings the next. There are no silos because there is no org chart yet.

The concrete goal was the first 1,000 marquee customers in the region. Not any 1,000 customers: companies whose names open doors. That detail matters. Early customers don't just pay; they define the category the market puts you in. Choose well and each one sells for you.

Then came Colombia, which proved the model could be repeated. In two years we reached the top 5 in transactional recognition in the corporate credit card market and passed 2,000 customers, with partners like Holcim, Avianca LifeMiles and Fendipetróleo. Today Clara is top 3 in commercial cards in Mexico and Colombia, competing head-on with Amex, Bancolombia and BBVA.

What I took from that stage comes down to three ideas I still use:

Be first. In a new market, getting there early lets you write the rules: what customers expect, what good service looks like, what a fair price is.

Move fast. Early on there isn't enough data to decide with certainty. You decide with what you have, measure and correct. Slowness costs more than mistakes.

Scale what works. Not everything you test deserves to grow. The discipline is spotting what does work, putting all your weight behind it, and dropping the rest without nostalgia.

If you are building growth from zero, don't start with the team or the tools. Start with the first customers you want to be able to name a year from now. Everything else falls into place around that.
